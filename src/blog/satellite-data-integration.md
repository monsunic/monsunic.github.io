Modern satellites continuously observe the world’s oceans — capturing wave height, wind vectors, and temperature fields in near real time. For regions with sparse in-situ networks, satellites are often the difference between a blind forecast and a well-constrained one.

Monsun assimilates satellite datasets into physical ocean models and monitoring products to improve marine forecasting accuracy, especially across Southeast Asian seas.

## Why satellite data matters for metocean intelligence

Buoys and coastal stations are gold-standard observations, but they are sparse. Satellites provide broad spatial coverage that helps:

- Correct large-scale wave and wind biases
- Detect synoptic features approaching a worksite
- Fill gaps between model update cycles
- Support validation of regional forecast systems

Used well, satellite inputs strengthen both consultancy-grade climate studies and day-to-day operational awareness.

## Satellite sources Monsun uses

- **Sentinel-3 and Jason-class altimetry** for sea surface height and derived wave information
- **ASCAT** and related scatterometers for ocean surface wind vectors
- **MODIS and VIIRS** for ocean color and sea surface temperature context

Combining these inputs enables more precise short-term marine condition predictions when fused with numerical models and local constraints.

## Assimilation and practical delivery

Data assimilation is the technical process of blending observations into models. Operationally, clients experience the result as better forecasts and monitoring: fewer surprises, clearer confidence notes, and products that track reality more closely during monsoon transitions and storm passages.

Monsun’s delivery options include dashboards, APIs, and consultant interpretation so satellite-enhanced intelligence reaches decision makers — not only modeling teams.

## Limitations to keep in mind

Satellites have revisit times, rain contamination issues for some sensors, and coastal retrieval challenges. Honest metocean consultancy acknowledges those limits and designs sensing strategies that mix satellite, model, and in-situ sources.
