Coastal zones are among the most dynamic and vulnerable environments on Earth. Rising development pressure, extreme weather, and ecosystem change all demand better information. Open marine datasets give governments, researchers, and operators a shared foundation for coastal management — when the data are curated and applied carefully.

Monsun integrates open sources into dashboards and forecast services so public and research users can act on ocean intelligence without starting from scratch.

## Why open ocean data matters

High-quality proprietary campaigns are valuable, but they cannot cover every coastline. Open data expands access to:

- Regional current, temperature, and salinity fields
- Wave model output for planning and education
- Bathymetry needed for inundation and navigation studies

For coastal managers, open data lowers the barrier to first-pass risk screening and supports transparent decision-making.

## Recommended open datasets

### Copernicus Marine Service

A cornerstone for ocean currents, temperature, salinity, and biogeochemistry. Useful for coastal monitoring frameworks and research baselines.

### NOAA WaveWatch III

Global and regional wave model products that support marine forecasting awareness and historical wave climate screening.

### GEBCO

High-resolution bathymetry essential for coastal modeling, flood pathways, and habitat context.

Monsun regularly combines these sources with local observations so products remain relevant to Southeast Asian coasts and ports.

## From open data to usable products

Raw datasets rarely answer operational questions on their own. Coastal teams need:

- Clear maps and time series
- Threshold-based alerts where appropriate
- Documentation of uncertainty and update cadence

That translation layer is where metocean consultancy skills matter. Monsun focuses on making open data usable for planning, education, and early-warning style awareness — complementing higher-fidelity commercial services when projects require them.

## Practical coastal use cases

Open data supports flood mitigation planning, port approach awareness, ecosystem monitoring, and public education through platforms such as Monsun Labs. When paired with local stakeholders, open ocean intelligence becomes a community asset rather than a specialist archive.
