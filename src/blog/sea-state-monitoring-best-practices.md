Sea state monitoring is the continuous observation and analysis of ocean surface conditions — typically waves, currents, and water levels — so marine operations can respond as conditions evolve. Unlike design studies that look decades ahead, monitoring is about the next minutes to days.

For Monsun clients, sea state monitoring is a core metocean service that complements forecasting and consultancy.

## What good sea state monitoring includes

Effective monitoring blends multiple sources:

- **In-situ sensors** such as wave buoys and ADCPs for ground truth
- **Radar and satellite** products for wider spatial coverage
- **Nowcast and short-range models** to fill gaps between observations
- **Clear dashboards** that highlight threshold breaches

The point is not to collect every possible dataset. It is to present the few variables that matter for safety and workability.

## Best practices for offshore teams

### 1. Define thresholds before you monitor

Agree on significant wave height, peak period, current speed, and visibility limits with vessel masters and project engineers. Monitoring without thresholds creates noise.

### 2. Validate models against local observations

Even strong global models can bias in coastal or island regions. Compare forecasts and nowcasts to nearby buoys or temporary sensors during the first weeks of a campaign.

### 3. Prefer decision-ready products

Operations teams need go / no-go clarity. Monsun designs monitoring products that highlight exceedances, timing of weather windows, and confidence notes — not only raw spectra.

### 4. Plan for communication failure modes

Offshore connectivity can drop. Build redundancy into delivery (satellite messaging, scheduled briefings, cached dashboards) so monitoring remains usable when networks fail.

### 5. Close the loop after each operation

Record when decisions were made and how conditions verified afterward. That history improves both monitoring and predictive analytics for the next campaign.
